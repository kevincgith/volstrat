# VTS Splits One Barometer Three Ways

VTS is essentially what you thought, with one correction. Its core "Total Portfolio Solution" is **three sleeves (Defensive Rotation, Tactical Volatility, Strategic Tail Risk)**. Each sleeve is 100% in one asset at a time and moves between an aggressive, a safety and a crisis position. Only Defensive Rotation trades the main 13-metric Volatility Barometer directly. The other two use **re-weighted variants that are "about 75% the same"**, not signal subsets. The sleeves also differ in what they hold: 2x Nasdaq-100 (QLD) in one, short volatility (SVXY) in another, the S&P 500 in the third. Re-entry is staggered on purpose. The stock-market-tilted sleeve comes back first, while the volatility sleeve waits for VIX futures to return to "a healthy level of contango". Signals go out once a day in a late-morning email, and subscribers fill at their own discretion. Almost none of the performance can be checked. Returns are published only as images. The rules, the sleeve count (5 to 4 to 3) and the defensive assets have all changed over time. The signal history behind the record starts in 2011, mostly as a backtest. The only independent evidence is a handful of unverified user claims, and the most specific one puts live 2018–2024 CAGR at **8.90% against SPY's 10.49%**. Your sense that the record looks "too good" is reasonable. It most likely combines early-year results under rules that were fitted later, a 2012–2017 short-volatility tailwind and unstated fill assumptions. It is not proof of a free lunch. For VolStrat, the cheap version of the VTS idea (sleeves built on signal subsets) has already been tested and gave the same return with slightly smaller drawdowns. The parts still worth testing are the ones VolStrat has not tried. The most important is that VTS's fast re-entry sleeve *underweights* term structure, the opposite of the fast sleeve VolStrat tested.

## Three sleeves trade three re-weighted barometers, not signal subsets

The current portfolio page says the Total Portfolio "utilizes all 3 of our strategies" and lists an optional Bitcoin Breakpoint strategy separately. The same page also refers to "all 5 tactical strategies", and the subscribe page sells "All 4 tactical strategy trades", so VTS's own pages disagree on the count ([VTS Portfolio](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/portfolio); [VTS Subscribe](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/subscribe)). The roster has changed over time. In February 2022 the portfolio was **"4 strategies with equal 25% weighting"**, each "an independent tactical strategy on its own" ([VTS, Feb 2022](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/article-635-5-volatility-ranges-and-our-expected-portfolio-allocations-during-each)). A 2023 third-party review listed **five sleeves at 20% each**, adding a Volatility Trend sleeve (VXX butterflies) and an SPY Iron Condor sleeve ([OptionsTradingIQ](https://optionstradingiq.com/vts-review/)). By August 2024 VTS was describing exactly three tactical strategies ([VTS, Aug 2024](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/tactical-investing-through-volatility-targeting)). No current sleeve weights or rebalancing rule appear in readable text. Equal thirds is the natural extrapolation from earlier practice, but nothing confirms it. For small accounts the FAQ suggests half in Tactical Volatility and half in one of the other two sleeves ([VTS FAQ](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/faq)).

The October 2026 post "3 Point Diversification with 3 different Volatility Barometers" states the current design most clearly. Defensive Rotation "uses the Volatility Barometer value exactly". Tactical Volatility runs on a variant that is about 75% the same and overweights "the VIX Futures Roll Yield and a few other metrics". Strategic Tail Risk runs on a variant that "focuses more on the mainstream stock market metrics, reduces roll yield and overweights Adjusted VIX futures" ([VTS, Oct 15 2026](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/3-point-diversification-with-3-different-volatility-barometers)). The mapping was different in February 2023. Then, Strategic Tail Risk used the full Barometer directly, Defensive Rotation used 5–6 metrics weighted toward VX30:VIX roll yield and Adjusted M1:M2, and a "Tactical Balanced" sleeve leaned on the Cash VIX Oscillator ([VTS, Feb 2023](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/3-vts-strategies-with-3-different-volatility-influences)). Which sleeve trades the composite has therefore moved between sleeves. The general idea has stayed the same: three overlapping composites, each with its own thresholds and its own assets, so the sleeves do not all switch on the same day.

Two details in the table need care. First, Defensive Rotation's rule as fetched goes to **cash when the Barometer is below 20%**, meaning it de-risks at extreme calm as well as at extreme stress. That fits VTS's remark that some inputs act as "contrarian signals in very low vol" ([VTS, May 2025](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/small-change-in-our-vts-volatility-barometer-methodology)). It came through a fetch summary, though, so check it against the original post before relying on it. Second, the instrument history shows leverage being reduced. Strategic Tail Risk held 2x S&P (SSO) in 2023, then unlevered SPY ("There's no leverage involved here") in 2024, then the low-beta single stock KO in October 2026 ([OptionsTradingIQ](https://optionstradingiq.com/vts-review/); [VTS, Aug 2024](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/tactical-investing-through-volatility-targeting); [VTS, Oct 2026](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/3-point-diversification-with-3-different-volatility-barometers)). The defensive leg also moved from VGLT, XLU and cash toward gold. The FAQ says "we don't short anything or use any undefined risk options" ([VTS FAQ](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/faq)). That is true only in a narrow sense: short volatility is taken by *buying* inverse ETPs, which still carry overnight gap risk.

## A 13-metric volatility percentile drives every switch, with no trend or credit input

The Barometer "takes information from 13 different Volatility metrics and combines it into a single value." It is built on rolling percentiles of data from 2006, and because it needs five years to rank, its **official inception is January 2011**. Before a rescale in May 2025 it ranged from about 13% to just over 90%. Its long-run average is **46.62%**: 39.94% in the seven years before the February 2018 "Volpocalypse" and 53.31% in the seven years after ([VTS, Feb 2025](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/volatility-regime-change-and-our-volatility-barometer)). From 2011 it spent **18.20% of days in its upper third and 30.40% in its lower third**. Above 80%, the strategies "may in fact be taking net short equity" / net long volatility ([VTS, Aug 2024](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/vts-volatility-barometer-levels-per-year-since-2011)). The May 2025 rescale let the reading reach 100% (Covid). VTS calls this "purely visual" and says signals use rolling percentiles that the change does not affect ([VTS, May 2025](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/small-change-in-our-vts-volatility-barometer-methodology)).

Only four input families are named publicly: VIX futures, cash VIX values, "Traders VRP" and the Cash VIX Oscillator ([VTS, May 2025](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/small-change-in-our-vts-volatility-barometer-methodology)). The wider 20-metric dashboard includes VX30:VIX roll yield, where VX30 is a constant one-month blend of M1 and M2 and the yield is (VX30 − VIX)/VIX ([VTS Dashboard Pt 6](https://volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/vts-volatility-dashboard-part-6-vx30-vix-adjusted-roll-yield)). It also includes four cash-VIX crossovers (VIX9D:VIX, VIX:VIX3M, VIX:VIX6M, VIX:VIX1Y) and a Cash VIX Oscillator that combines horizons from nine days to one year using an unpublished formula ([VTS CVO post](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/vts-cash-vix-oscillator-volatility-dashboard-metric)). The other nine inputs, all weights and the exact percentile window are paywalled or unpublished. The inputs that *are* known differ from VolStrat's in an important way. **No public VTS post describes an equity trend, credit spread or put/call input.** The Barometer is a pure volatility-complex measure, tuned to the roll dynamics of vol ETPs. Even Strategic Tail Risk's "mainstream stock market metrics" are not named.

Execution is loose. One email goes out each trading day before noon ET, typically about 11:15. It lists each sleeve's signals and "allocation sizes", and subscribers may trade at any time that day ([VTS FAQ](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/faq)). No smoothing, confirmation period or hysteresis band is published. The thresholds are bare levels, and the 2023 Strategic Tail Risk rule was a one-point flip: "If it says 54% we will be in the S&P 500", while 56% meant cash ([VTS, Feb 2023](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/3-vts-strategies-with-3-different-volatility-influences)). VTS also does not say which price its own record uses for fills.

## Re-entry is staggered by design, and the vol sleeve is deliberately slow

VTS does not wait for every signal to turn risk-on. Each sleeve re-enters when its own composite crosses its own level, and the composites are weighted so they recover at different speeds. After the April 2025 spike VTS described the sequence directly. Strategic Tail Risk does not "have to wait until the VIX futures are well into Contango". Defensive Rotation waits "slightly longer to get back in, just in case there are follow up dips". Tactical Volatility waits "until the VIX futures term structure is back into a healthy level of Contango", because SVXY can fall much further than stocks. At that point SPY was about 5% from its high, QLD about 20% and SVXY still about 55% from breaking even ([VTS, May 2025](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/short-volatility-svxy-can-crash-a-lot-more-than-stocks)). Defensive Rotation's mechanical re-entry is the Barometer falling back below 66%. That bar is wide: going by the 2011–2024 distribution, Defensive Rotation should be in 2x Nasdaq on roughly three-quarters or more of trading days. That is an inference from the zone statistics, not a disclosed figure.

The point that matters most for VolStrat is this. **VTS's fastest re-entry sleeve is the one that underweights roll yield and term structure, and its slowest is the one that overweights them.** VTS treats term-structure normalisation as a reason to stay *out*, and keeps it for the short-vol sleeve, where re-entering late costs little. VTS's own description of past crashes shows the same lag VolStrat sees. After 2011 it was "almost 2 months after the market bottomed" before the Barometer stayed below 60%. Mar 2020 took "several months to normalize", and Feb 2018 "a long time". Recovery was quick in 2022 and 2023, and in August 2024 it was close to 60% five days after the bottom. VTS says it optimises "for what happens 9 out of 10 times" ([VTS, crashes post](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/vts-volatility-barometer-through-all-market-crashes)). So VTS has not solved the slow-rebound problem in the largest V-shaped recoveries. It spreads re-entry across sleeves and accepts that the vol sleeve misses the early rebound.

On de-risking, VTS gives one example. Tactical Volatility got its signal to move to gold on July 24, 2024, about nine days before the August 2 VXX spike, and Defensive Rotation moved to XLU "way ahead of time" ([VTS, Aug 2024](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/tactical-investing-through-volatility-targeting)). VTS also says its vol strategies were in cash before Volpocalypse ([VTS CVO post](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/vts-cash-vix-oscillator-volatility-dashboard-metric)). Both claims are self-reported. VTS also admits the strategy needs "a little bit of weakness to get the signals to move to safety" ([VTS, Oct 2024](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/tactical-volatility-strategy-vs-svxy-in-2024)). A midday email cannot get a subscriber out ahead of an overnight shock such as the after-hours collapse of short-vol ETPs on February 5, 2018 or the Asia-session spike on August 5, 2024.

## The headline return cannot be verified and rests on shifting rules

**What VTS itself reports.** Performance is "all live trades" from the daily emails, including fees and dividends, but the figures appear only as images on the portfolio page ([VTS Portfolio](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/portfolio)). VTS launched in January 2012 ([VTS FAQ](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/faq)). The readable self-reported numbers are fragments. In January 2024 the Total Portfolio had a 50% correlation with the S&P 500 and was "still returning over 20% in a bull market", and Iron Condor had returned "around 17% Annualized since January 2012" ([VTS Correlation Matrix](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/correlation-matrix-of-all-our-vts-strategies)). Tactical Volatility returned **+0.85% with an 8.70% max drawdown from July 12 to October 23, 2024, against SVXY's −23.07% and 38.48%**, over a short window VTS chose itself ([VTS, Oct 2024](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/blog/tactical-volatility-strategy-vs-svxy-in-2024)). The disclaimer is boilerplate and says nothing about backtested versus actual results, fill prices or slippage ([VTS Disclaimer](https://www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com/disclaimer)).

**What third parties claim, all unverified.** A commenter on the 2023 review says his own CAGR following VTS was **8.90% from January 2018 to October 2024, against SPY 10.49% and VBINX 8.05%**. Other commenters say the site chart is backtested and that critical comments were removed ([OptionsTradingIQ comments](https://optionstradingiq.com/vts-review/)). Trustpilot has three unverified reviews averaging 2.8. One five-star review says the service avoided the 2018 and 2020 crashes. One one-star reviewer reports losing about **40% from September 2022, while VTS reported about −19%** for the same period, and says VTS refused to show the trade prices. Another one-star reviewer says the operator "only did simulated accounts" ([Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/www.volatilitytradingstrategies.com)). A Bogleheads poster, seen only in a search snippet because the page returned 403, says the 24% CAGR is "highly dependent on gains" from 2012–2016 and that VTS stopped reporting individual strategies ([Bogleheads](https://www.bogleheads.org/forum/viewtopic.php?t=324670)). There is no Collective2 record, no audited record and no broker-linked record.

**What is unknown or paywalled:** the year-by-year returns and drawdowns for each sleeve and for the total, the historical daily Barometer values, the Tactical Volatility and Strategic Tail Risk thresholds, nine of the 13 inputs and all weights, the current sleeve weights and rebalancing rule, the fill convention, and what VTS actually held going into February 5, 2018, March 2020, August 5, 2024 and April 2025.

Your "aggressive when calm, absent in crashes" reading fits the rules, and the rules alone explain much of it. Under equal thirds, the calm state holds about **one-third QLD (≈0.67x Nasdaq-100 notional), one-third SVXY and one-third SPY**. That is roughly 1.0x equity notional plus a short-volatility carry sleeve. It was more aggressive in 2023, when SSO, QLD, a short-vol sleeve, VXX butterflies and condors made up the bundle. In stress, the sleeves can rotate to cash, gold and long volatility. That last step is something VolStrat cannot do, and it can make money during crashes rather than merely holding cash. The headline is still not credible as stated, for four reasons. **The signal history from 2011 is a backtest,** and percentile-ranked inputs plus thresholds chosen later fit that history in-sample. **The rules, roster and defensive assets kept changing,** so a continuous curve "since 2012" cannot come from one rule set run live. **The 2012–2017 period strongly favoured short volatility,** and the one specific live claim for 2018 onwards trails SPY. **With fills at subscriber discretion and no stated convention,** reported results for vol ETPs can differ a lot from what subscribers actually got. That is what the −40% versus −19% complaint alleges. My judgment is that the structure is coherent and the drawdown control is plausible. The return level is unproven, and any return above equity levels most likely comes from 2012–2017 and from short-vol carry, not from timing skill that persisted after 2018.

## VolStrat has already tested the cheap version of the VTS idea

The two systems share a skeleton: percentile-ranked inputs, a composite score and step-function exposure. Beyond that they differ in ways that matter.

| Dimension | VTS | VolStrat (Growth) |
|---|---|---|
| Inputs | 13 volatility-complex metrics; no public trend or credit | VIX, VIX/VIX3M, VX slope, VVIX, SKEW, put/call, VRP, HY-vs-IEF, S&P vs 200-day |
| Normalisation | Rolling percentiles, 5-year warm-up, history from 2011 | 3-year window, 5-day EMA |
| Construction | 3 sleeves, each with its own composite, thresholds and assets | 1 score, 5 regimes, panic override |
| Calm exposure | ≈1.0x equity notional plus short-vol carry (inferred) | 125% (Risk-On) to 200% (Strong Risk-On) equity notional |
| Crisis asset | Cash, gold, long VIX futures | BIL; Risk-Off is IEF/GLD/XLU |
| Execution | Midday email, discretionary fills | Next close, 5 bp |
| Out-of-era evidence | None before 2011 | 1993–2006: 5.6% CAGR, Sharpe 0.18, max DD −50.1% |

VolStrat figures: VolStrat backtests, this session, data to 2026-11-03.

Two things stand out. First, **VolStrat is the more leveraged system on equity in calm markets.** VTS's apparent extra return in calm markets comes from a different risk premium (short-vol carry) and a leveraged Nasdaq tilt, not from more S&P leverage. Second, the "three barometers" idea is close to something VolStrat has already tested. Seven three-sleeve blends of signal subsets gave **9.1–9.8% CAGR against 9.6% for the single score**. The best blend, which combined the full score, a vol-level sleeve (VIX, VVIX, VRP) and a non-vol sleeve (trend, credit, put/call, SKEW), reached **9.8% CAGR, Sharpe 0.68 and max DD −21.4%**, against Growth's 9.6%, 0.66 and −23.8%. It traded about 2.5 times as often, but cost drag rose only from 0.67% to 0.69%. From 1993 to 2006 the blends cut the max drawdown from −50.1% to between −41% and −44%, but still lagged 60/40 (VolStrat backtests, this session, data to 2026-11-03). That non-vol sleeve is close to VTS's "mainstream stock market metrics" sleeve. So VolStrat's own data already gives the answer the literature predicts: averaging step functions mostly lowers risk and luck rather than adding return. The cross-sectional debate about mixing versus integrating signals is unresolved: [Ghayur, Heaney & Platt](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2018/faj-v74-n3-5) find the winner depends on active risk, and [Leippold & Rüegg](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/eufman/v24y2018i5p829-855.html) find no robust edge for integration. In practice, ensembles across rules and rebalance dates mainly remove timing luck ([Newfound](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2019/07/ensemble-multi-asset-momentum/); [Hoffstein, Sibears & Faber](https://www.thinknewfound.com/rebalance-timing-luck)).

The fast-sleeve result also makes sense now. VolStrat's fast sleeve was built on VIX/VIX3M and VX slope with no smoothing, and it lost money: CAGR 4.7% on its own, with 4.0% a year of cost drag. Two-thirds Growth plus one-third fast gave 8.1% CAGR, Sharpe 0.55 and max DD −23.4%. The term-plus-VIX-level variant reached 8.7% and 0.59, and beat Growth's 2017–2026 Sharpe in only 20% of bootstrap resamples. Its equity position ten days after the low matched Growth's in 9 of 10 major lows (VolStrat backtests, this session, data to 2026-11-03). That sleeve was fast in the wrong dimension. VTS treats term structure as the *slowest* signal to normalise, which is what VolStrat found. Research gives the same warning: VIX-futures backwardation works as a *contrarian* equity buy signal, and contango carries no timing information ([Fassas & Hourvouliades](https://mdpi.com/1911-8074/12/3/113)). The basis predicts VIX futures returns rather than spot VIX ([Simon & Campasano](https://www.efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2013-Reading/papers/EFMA2013_0164_fullpaper.pdf)). The rebound problem itself is large. Growth's equity was zero ten days after most major lows, and it captured a median of about 29% of the S&P's three-month rebound across ten lows (five lows at 6–17%, five at 41–59%; an earlier draft said 41%, which was the upper of the two middle values). In March 2020 the S&P rose 39.9% over three months while Growth rose 2.3% (VolStrat backtests, this session, data to 2026-11-03). But VTS has no proven fix either: its own Barometer took months to normalise after 2011, 2018 and 2020. The one VolStrat re-entry rule that has worked is the capitulation-conditioned panic override (test Sharpe +0.09, 92% of resamples). Re-entering on ordinary price or volatility easing failed and bought 2008 bear-market rallies (earlier VolStrat research).

## Six tests worth running, and the bar each must clear

Five ideas from VTS have not yet been tested in VolStrat, plus one test that uses VolStrat's data to check VTS's credibility. Each should be specified in full before it is run. Each should be scored on 2007–2026, on 1993–2006 wherever the inputs exist, and with a block bootstrap of the Sharpe difference. A test should count as a pass only if it improves Sharpe in at least about 80% of resamples, roughly the evidence the panic rule had, and does not worsen 1993–2006. Report the median across ±5-point threshold jitter, not the best setting. With about ten major lows since 2007, any rule fitted to rebound timing has only a few independent data points. A new rule's outcome in March 2020 or April 2025 is therefore close to anecdote.

**Test 1: a fast re-entry sleeve that underweights term structure, gated on capitulation.** Copy VTS's ordering: build the fast sleeve from VIX *level*, VRP, credit and trend, with VIX/VIX3M and VX slope weighted at zero. Let it re-enter only after the panic condition (VIX/VIX3M above 1.10 within 20 days) has fired, so it cannot repeat the 2008 bear-rally failure. Hold it at one-third weight beside Growth. The pass bar has three parts: median three-month rebound capture rises clearly above about 29%, the 2008 drawdown is no deeper than Growth's own 2008 drawdown, and the bootstrap bar is met.

**Test 1 result (run 2026-11-04, data to 2026-11-03): fails.** Fast sleeve: VIX level, VRP, credit and trend at weight 1, no smoothing, VIX/VIX3M and VX slope off. Gate: a post-panic window opens when VIX/VIX3M exceeds 1.10 and closes when Growth's own regime (without the rebound override) reaches Neutral, or after 126 days. Inside it the sleeve takes the higher of its own regime and Growth's; outside it copies Growth. At 1/3 weight with unshifted cut-offs: CAGR 9.5% vs 9.6%, Sharpe 0.65 vs 0.66, max DD unchanged at −23.8%, 2008–09 drawdown −19.4% vs −18.8%, median rebound capture 30.3% vs 28.9%, Sharpe better than Growth in 16% of resamples (2017–2026: 16%). Across cut-off shifts of −5/0/+5 and weights of 1/3 and 1/2, capture ranged 26–34% and the bootstrap win rate 10–42%. The window was open on 959 days, but the sleeve sat above Growth on only 48: VIX level, VRP and trend recover as slowly as the composite, so no part of the existing signal set is fast after a low. March 2020 capture was unchanged (5.8%). Before 2006 the gate cannot fire (no VIX3M), so 1993–2006 is identical to Growth. The only signal that normalises quickly after capitulation is the term structure itself, which the panic-rebound rule already uses; Test 2 is the remaining lever.

**Test 2: re-entry speeds staggered inside the panic rule.** VolStrat's panic override currently lifts the whole portfolio. Split it across three sleeves that release at different points: sleeve 1 when VIX/VIX3M falls below 1.00 (today's rule), sleeve 2 when it falls below 0.95, and sleeve 3 only when the composite itself recovers. This is the cleanest copy of VTS's "staggered re-entry" with the fewest new parameters. Judge it against the single panic rule, not against Growth.

**Test 3: sleeves that differ in thresholds and assets, not just inputs.** VTS's sleeves diversify mainly because they hold different assets and switch at different levels. VolStrat's blends shared one asset map. Re-run the best blend with each sleeve's regime cutoffs offset (−5, 0 and +5 score points) and, separately, with a piecewise-linear interpolation between regime portfolios in place of the step map. Then check whether the blend's mean return beats the average of its components' returns, net of turnover. If it does, the gain comes from the step-function nonlinearity and not only from diversification. That is the condition the literature identifies for blending to *add* return ([Goulding, Harvey & Mazzoleni](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:149:y:2023:i:3:p:378-406)).

**Test 4: a complacency cap.** Check VTS's below-20% cash rule against the original post first. Then test one setting fixed in advance: when the VolStrat score is above its own 95th percentile (expanding window), cap Strong Risk-On at Risk-On. The prior is low, and VolStrat has few tops to test on. Treat a pass on 2007–2026 alone as unproven.

**Test 5: a short-vol carry sleeve, as a research question only.** This is VTS's most distinctive return source and the one VolStrat lacks entirely. Test it at no more than 10% weight in SVXY. Enter only when VX30:VIX roll yield is positive *and* VolStrat is at Neutral or better, with BIL as the exit. Assume a full open-to-open fill on shock days, and model February 5, 2018 explicitly, since the after-hours loss cannot be avoided. If it adds Sharpe only by taking on crash risk that has not yet shown up in the sample, reject it.

**Test 6 (credibility check): run VTS's Defensive Rotation rule on VolStrat's score.** Map (100 − VolStrat score) through VTS's 20/66/85 bands into cash, QLD, XLU and cash from 2007. This is only a rough proxy, because the inputs differ. But if a rule that is in 2x Nasdaq most days produces strong returns mainly through the QLD tilt, that shows how much of VTS's headline comes from instrument choice rather than signal quality.

**What each result says.**
- **Re-entry (Tests 1–2).** No signal in the existing set recovers quickly after a low except the VIX curve, and the panic rule already uses it. Staggering the release only interpolates between today's rule and slower ones. A single earlier release (VIX/VIX3M below 1.05) wins in 90–93% of 2017–2026 resamples but only 16–37% in 2007–2016, across cut-off shifts of −5/0/+5. That is an era effect, not a robust edge.
- **Blending (Test 3).** Each blend's mean return beat the average of its own sleeves by only 0.01–0.09% a year in 2007–2026. So the step map is not costing return through its nonlinearity, and blending is diversification, nothing more. The one notable result is that the linear map clearly helped 1993–2006 (Sharpe 0.18 → 0.27, max DD −50.1% → −42.7%, 96%) while losing in 2017–2026 (2%). It needs a reason fixed in advance before it becomes a candidate, not another search.
- **Complacency cap (Test 4).** Every Strong Risk-On day (97 since 2007, 41 in 1993–2006) was above the 95th percentile, so the cap simply removes Strong Risk-On. The small 2007–2016 gain is not repeated in either other period.
- **Short-vol carry (Test 5).** The synthetic short-term VIX futures index rose 97.4% on 2018-02-05. The sleeve avoided that day only because VX30:VIX roll yield turned negative at the 2018-02-01 close; on 2018-01-31 it still read +0.3%. A 10% sleeve held through Feb 5 would have cost the portfolio about 4.9% (−0.5x) or 9.7% (−1x). All of the gain comes from 2007–2016, and the sleeve lowered Sharpe in 87–96% of 2017–2026 resamples. This matches the 2012–2017 short-vol tailwind behind VTS's early record.
- **Credibility check (Test 6).** VTS's bands put VolStrat's score in 2x tech on 75% of days. That gives 18.5% CAGR, but a −63.9% max drawdown and a −55.4% 2008–09 drawdown, against −82.5% for buy-and-hold 2x XLK, which earned 23.5% a year. With a signal of VolStrat's quality, a VTS-style rotation earns its return from the leveraged tech instrument and does *not* avoid the big drawdowns. For VTS's record to show both, its Barometer would have to exit crashes far better than any signal tested here. That is possible, but it is an extraordinary claim with no independent record behind it. Caveat: the VTS Barometer is a different series from VolStrat's score, so this is a proxy.

Synthetic series used: short-vol = −0.5x (or −1x) the daily M1/M2 rolling VIX futures return from CFE settlements, plus T-bill interest, minus 0.95% a year; 2x XLK = twice XLK's daily total return, minus T-bills plus 0.75%, minus 0.95% a year. Scripts: the session scratchpad (`sleeves/test1.ts`, `test2.ts`, `test3456.ts`).

## Conclusion

VTS's main idea is useful but different from how it appears from outside. Its sleeves do not get their edge from splitting signals. They hold different assets, which re-enter at deliberately different speeds, and VTS accepts that its volatility sleeve will miss the early rebound. VolStrat's own tests already show that splitting signals alone buys a slightly smaller drawdown and no extra return. The one VTS idea that VolStrat has tested the wrong way round is which signals should drive fast re-entry. VTS keeps term structure for the slow sleeve. VolStrat's failed fast sleeve was built almost entirely from it.

On credibility, the useful question is not whether VTS's chart can be trusted. It is which parts of its return a disciplined backtest could reproduce. The likely answer: drawdown reduction from volatility-based rotation, which is robust as risk control but unreliable as alpha out of sample ([Cederburg et al.](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)), plus short-vol carry and a leveraged Nasdaq tilt that suited 2012–2017 and the years after. VolStrat already has the first. Its weak 1993–2006 result is a reminder that any system calibrated only on the post-2008 volatility regime, as VTS's has been since 2011, has never been tested in a different era.
