# Sell VolStrat as a Risk Dial, Not Alpha

The research literature and VolStrat's own record say the same thing. Volatility and risk-regime signals reliably reshape risk: thinner left tails, smaller drawdowns, steadier volatility. They are unreliable at raising risk-adjusted returns once tests are run in real time, at equal risk and after costs. In the largest out-of-sample study, volatility-managed versions of 103 equity strategies had a higher gross Sharpe ratio in only 53 cases, just 8 of them significantly, and adding them to a mean-variance mix *lowered* Sharpe for 72 of the 103 ([Cederburg et al. 2020](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)). Regime-switching and trend studies win mainly by avoiding large losses ([Kritzman, Page & Turkington 2012](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2012/regime-shifts-implications-for-dynamic-strategies-corrected)). Institutions build their gauges from the same inputs VolStrat uses, implied volatility and credit spreads, and use them to decide *how much* risk to hold, not to forecast returns. VolStrat fits this pattern closely. Growth earns about the same return as a static mix with the same 79% average equity (9.6% vs 9.5% CAGR) with roughly half the drawdown (−23.8% vs −44.2%). It trailed 60/40 in 1993–2006 (Sharpe 0.18 vs 0.51), and the v3 redesign only tied its benchmark in the European holdout (0.25 vs 0.25) (VolStrat backtests, data to 2026-11-03). Its score sorts future volatility and tail odds but not future returns (VolStrat METHODOLOGY §11). The tool should therefore stop presenting itself as an allocator that beats benchmarks. It should become a transparent risk-regime dashboard plus an overlay that sets a risk budget on a PM's own strategic portfolio, judged on information ratio at equal risk, false-alarm cost and drawdown saved per unit of return given up. Most of the needed machinery already exists in the repo. What is missing is the overlay mode, probability and transition outputs, tranching, and a shift in headline claims.

## Real-time tests shrink volatility-timing alpha to noise but keep the tail cut

The case for volatility timing started strong in sample. Moreira and Muir scaled monthly exposure by the inverse of last month's realized variance and reported **4.9% annualized alpha and about a 25% higher Sharpe ratio for the market** ([NBER w22208](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208)). Later work took most of that apart. Their scaling constant was calibrated on full-sample volatility, which no investor could have known in advance. Re-estimating it in real time with fixed or rolling 5–20-year windows produced **maximum drawdowns of 68%–93%**, against 56% for the look-ahead version and about 50% for the market, and the strategy beat the market only around the financial crisis ([Liu, Tang & Zhou 2019](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/volatility-managed-portfolio-does-it-really-work/)). Net of trading costs, volatility management of equity factors other than the market earns no abnormal return and lowers Sharpe ratios ([Barroso & Detzel 2021](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:140:y:2021:i:3:p:744-767)). Cederburg et al.'s 103-strategy study traces the out-of-sample failure to instability in the scaling parameters ([RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:138:y:2020:i:1:p:95-117)).

Risk shaping is what survives. Across 60+ assets with data back to 1926, Man AHL's study found that volatility targeting **consistently lowers the probability of extreme returns and the volatility of volatility for every asset class**. For equities, the standard deviation of rolling one-year realized volatility fell from 4.6% to 1.8%. Sharpe ratios improved for equities and credit, but the effect was "negligible" for bonds, currencies and commodities ([Harvey et al. 2018](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/the-impact-of-volatility-targeting-on-equities-bonds-commodities-and-currencies)). A secondary summary puts the US equity Sharpe at 0.40 unscaled and 0.48–0.51 scaled. That figure has not been checked against the paper ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/volatility-targeting-improves-risk-adjusted-returns/)). The design detail that matters most comes from Bongaerts, Kang and van Dijk. Conventional volatility targeting "can lead to markedly greater drawdowns" in global equity markets. A conditional version that acts only in the extreme high- and low-volatility states improved Sharpe in every market tested, with less turnover and leverage ([CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2020/conditional-volatility-targeting)). Read with Liu, Tang and Zhou, the pattern is consistent. De-risking in high-volatility states is fairly robust. Levering up in calm states is where real-time versions get hurt.

There is also a reason why volatility predicts risk without predicting return. The variance risk premium (implied minus realized variance) predicts *higher* equity returns at roughly quarterly horizons after 1990 ([Bollerslev, Tauchen & Zhou 2009](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:22:y:2009:i:11:p:4463-4492)), and the effect holds across countries ([Fed FEDS 2011-52](https://www.federalreserve.gov/Pubs/feds/2011/201152/201152pap.pdf)). A rule that de-risks on high implied volatility therefore sells into the states with the highest expected returns. It is paid in lower risk, not higher return. VolStrat's diagnostics show exactly this. On Balanced parameters with S&P 500 data from 1993 to 2026, days classed as Crisis were followed by **annualized returns of 24.6% at 37.2% volatility**, and Risk-On days by 8.4% at 12.4%. The Sharpe ratios were almost identical (0.66 vs 0.68). The odds of a 10% loss over the next quarter were 10.4% against 3.4%, and the score's rank correlation with next-month returns was −0.06 (t = −1.2) (VolStrat METHODOLOGY §11, data to 2026-10-22). VIX term-structure signals are weaker still. Backwardation works as a *contrarian* buy signal, and contango carries no significant information ([Fassas & Hourvouliades, via Macrosynergy](https://macrosynergy.com/?p=41000)). A published VIX-basis strategy shows slightly negative out-of-sample returns in replication ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/exploiting-term-structure-of-vix-futures)).

Regime-switching and trend research reaches the same verdict by different routes. Kritzman, Page and Turkington fitted two-state Markov models to turbulence, inflation and growth. Their dynamic strategies beat static ones out of sample, "especially for investors who seek to avoid large losses" ([IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ufajxx/v68y2012i3p22-39.html)). An independent South African test found the same for a turbulence-regime TAA ([Flint & Maré 2019](https://ajol.info/index.php/saaj/article/view/191873/181084)). Adaptive HMMs survive costs only with trading penalties and a realistic delay ([Nystrup 2014](https://www2.imm.dtu.dk/pubdb/pubs/6808-full.html); [Nystrup et al. 2018](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:quantf:v:18:y:2018:i:1:p:83-95)), and later work frames the core problem as a trade-off between detection accuracy and latency ([Shu, Yu & Mulvey 2024](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2402.05272)). Trend has the longest record. Time-series momentum was positive in every decade since 1880 and did well in 8 of the 10 largest crises ([Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen 2017](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing)). Its statistical predictability is disputed ([Huang et al. 2020](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:135:y:2020:i:3:p:774-794)), and an S&P-only 10-month moving-average rule still showed a drawdown near −50% in one secondary test ([Faber, via Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/doc/116881446/SSRN-id962461)). The most useful paper for VolStrat is Goulding, Harvey and Mazzoleni. They split fast and slow momentum into four states (Bull, Correction, Bear and Rebound) and show that blending speeds handles turning points better than either speed alone ([JFE 2023](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:149:y:2023:i:3:p:378-406)). Their "Rebound" state (slow trend negative, fast trend positive) is exactly where VolStrat's v4 broke down. A two-century study, published as an abstract in January 2026, finds trend following and defensive equity factors the most effective protection and complementary across drawdown stages. Gold and puts were less cost-effective ([Baltussen, Martens & van der Linden, FAJ 2026](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2026/best-defensive-strategies)).

## Institutions build the same gauge and use it to size risk, not to forecast

The institutional tools are built in much the same way. SSGA's Market Regime Indicator combines equity implied volatility, currency implied volatility and risky-debt spreads into a 0–100% composite, with a separate regime label for each factor ([SSGA MRI Q1 2024](https://ssga.com/us/en/intermediary/insights/market-regime-indicator-q1-2024)). Citi's macro risk index uses credit spreads, FX volatility and equity volatility ([Interactive Advisors](https://investing.interactiveadvisors.com/2012/09/its-officially-a-risk-on-market)). The public indices are documented in full. The OFR Financial Stress Index uses **33 variables in 5 categories and 3 regions**, weighted by a dynamic factor model ([OFR](https://www.financialresearch.gov/financial-stress-index)). The Chicago Fed's NFCI weights **105 indicators** and publishes every weight; VIX's weight is 3.10, among the largest, next to spread series ([NFCI FAQ](https://www.chicagofed.org/-/media/publications/nfci/nfci-faqs-pdf.pdf)). The Kansas City Fed's risk-on/risk-off index is the first principal component of spreads, equity volatility and returns, funding liquidity, and gold and the dollar ([KC Fed](https://www.kansascityfed.org/data-and-trends/risk-on-risk-off-index/)). VolStrat's inputs (VIX, term structure, VVIX, SKEW, put/call, VRP, credit, trend) are this same common core. **The inputs are not a differentiator.** Any edge has to come from transparency, analytics and decision rules.

How institutions use these tools falls into a few documented patterns, and none of them is "follow the signal into a fixed portfolio". SSGA calls its MRI "a key driver of the active positioning" for broad directional bets. It typically underweights equities when risk aversion is high, and it combines the MRI with valuation, credit and momentum signals ([SSGA TAA paper](https://www.ssga.com/library-content/assets/pdf/global/pc/2025/why-institutions-should-consider-taa-gbl.pdf)). Kritzman and Li propose three uses for their turbulence index: stress-test with covariances from turbulent periods, build turbulence-resistant portfolios, and scale risk exposure when turbulence is high ([FAJ 2010](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2010/skulls-financial-turbulence-and-risk-management)). MSCI built a volatility-regime adjustment into its Barra risk models, so risk forecasts, and with them risk budgets and tracking-error limits, catch up faster when regimes change ([MSCI](https://ir.msci.com/news-releases/news-release-details/msci-launches-new-barra-equity-models)). Two Sigma suggests sampling from regime-conditional distributions for stress tests ([Two Sigma](https://www.twosigma.com/articles/a-machine-learning-approach-to-regime-modeling)). Even BofA's widely cited "sell signal" comes with advice to rotate into defensives and "no rush to cut exposure" ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/bofas-hartnett-sees-sell-signal-for-risk-assets-but-no-rush-to-cut-exposure-4706152)). In practice the regime gauge sets the size of a tilt or a risk budget, and it is used alongside other inputs.

The outputs the most rigorous providers publish are a useful spec for VolStrat. The common set is a continuous score with a discrete label, attribution by component or category (OFR by 5 categories and 3 regions, KC RORO's four [FRED sub-indices](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/KCRORO)), and a downloadable history. Two Sigma's Gaussian-mixture model goes further. It publishes **daily probabilities across four regimes** (Crisis, Steady State, Inflation, "Walking on Ice") and a regime-conditional table of factor means, volatilities and correlations. In the Walking-on-Ice state, Value volatility runs at 18.2% against 8.9% long-run ([Two Sigma](https://www.twosigma.com/articles/a-machine-learning-approach-to-regime-modeling)). Man Group and Duke take an analog approach. They z-score seven state variables, measure each month's distance from today, treat the most-similar 20% of months as the regime and the least-similar 20% as the anti-regime, and exclude the last 36 months. Factor timing on that basis produced a long-short Sharpe of 0.82 over 1985–2024 ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/regimes-systematic-models-power-of-prediction)). No public provider found in this research publishes a transition matrix or expected regime duration, even though Markov models imply both. That gap is a cheap way for VolStrat to stand out.

The documented failure modes are just as instructive. Popular risk-appetite indices "give contradictory signals in practice" and rest on little theory ([Misina, Bank of Canada WP 06-16](https://oar-rao.bank-banque-canada.ca/record/1366/files/wp06-16.pdf)). Components inside one tool also disagree: SSGA recorded credit spreads tightening while equity volatility rose in October 2023 ([SSGA MRI Q4 2023](https://www.ssga.com/library-content/assets/pdf/north-america/mas/2023/inst-pdf-market-regime-indicator-q4-2023.pdf)). NFCI history "can change from week to week" through data revisions, whereas an OFR value "does not change" once estimated ([NFCI FAQ](https://www.chicagofed.org/-/media/publications/nfci/nfci-faqs-pdf.pdf); [OFR WP 17-04](https://www.financialresearch.gov/working-papers/2017/10/25/the-ofr-financial-stability-index/)). Two Sigma calls its model "not predictive" and warns that labels switch abruptly on transient moves. Man concedes that picking state variables "itself induces look-ahead bias" and that COVID had no historical analogue. BofA's sell-signal track record is reported as 32 signals since 2002 in one outlet and 16 by December 2025 in another ([Ground News](https://ground.news/article/a-sell-signal-has-been-triggered-at-bank-of-america-heres-what-happens-next); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/12/19/stocks-bank-of-america-warns-contrarian-sell-signal)). No independent false-alarm study exists for the SSGA, Goldman or Citi indicators. VolStrat already has a frozen, append-only live record, non-revising data, published weights and pre-registered tests. Those are exactly the credibility features the sell-side tools lack, and they are worth more as a product than another backtest improvement.

## Overlays succeed on band width, tranching and equal-risk accounting

An overlay changes exposure around a strategic allocation that stays in place. UK local-authority pension papers define TAA as committee-approved deviations from the agreed strategic weights, often run through a derivatives overlay so the underlying managers are left untouched ([Hampshire CC](https://www.hants.gov.uk/decisions/decisions-docs/070525-penfun-r0521144011)). Large asset owners increasingly use a Total Portfolio Approach: a single reference portfolio plus an active-risk budget. CPP Investments measures value added against **85% global equities and 15% Canadian government bonds** ([Universal Asset Owners](https://www.universalassetowners.com/gic-vs-cpp/)), and CalPERS reportedly voted to adopt the approach from July 2026 ([iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/1177183732)). In that setting a regime signal is one budgeted source of active risk. Its question is "how far from the reference portfolio, in which direction, today?", not "which five portfolios should I rotate between?" The budget should be small. CEM data for 1992–2013 put pension funds' *total* active value added at about 58 bps a year gross and 16 bps net ([IPE](https://www.ipe.com/active-management-boosts-performance-by-16-basis-points-study-finds/10006836.article)), and TAA made up less than 1% of holdings ([Aglietta et al. 2012](https://www.scribd.com/document/490513995/aglietta2012-pdf)).

Implementation can matter as much as the signal. Newfound Research shows that the choice of rebalance date alone creates **100–400 bps a year of performance dispersion** in rule-based portfolios. Two otherwise identical momentum variants were down 50.3% and 13.8% at the same moment, and shifting a monthly multi-asset momentum model across 21 day-offsets moved its annualized return between 4.7% and 5.5%. The fix is tranching: overlapping sleeves that rebalance on staggered dates, or correcting a fraction of the drift each period ([Newfound](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/tag/rebalance-timing-luck/); [Newfound 2019](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2019/07/ensemble-multi-asset-momentum/)). VolStrat's own blending tests show the same thing from the other side. Three-sleeve blends beat the average of their own sleeves by only **0.01–0.09% a year**, so blending and staggering reduce luck and drawdown but add no return (VolStrat backtests, data to 2026-11-03). Taxes are the other implementation cost. Schwab models 5% turnover as costing about 71 bps a year after tax and 100% turnover as costing about 194 bps ([Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/resource/scfr-tax-drag)). Research Affiliates warns that TAA alpha "can be easily exceeded by the increased taxes that result from profitable turnover" ([RA](https://www.researchaffiliates.com/content/dam/ra/publications/pdf/713-tactical-and-tax-aware-gtaa.pdf)). With realistic TAA alpha in the tens of basis points, a taxable PM needs to see tax drag before acting on a signal.

The evidence on hedging points toward reducing equity beta, not buying protection. Israelov compares a put-protected portfolio with a "divested" one holding less equity at equal exposure. The divested portfolio "nearly universally has better drawdown characteristics", because the volatility risk premium makes puts expensive. Puts win mainly when a large crash happens before expiry ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/white-papers/pathetic-protection-the-elusive-benefits-of-protective-puts?aqrPDF=1)). Man's study of eight equity drawdowns found trend strategies protected well and puts protected at a large cost ([Harvey et al. 2019](https://www.scribd.com/document/491534322/jpm-2019-45-5-007)). Cboe's put-protection index cut the 37-year maximum drawdown only to **38.91% from 50.95%** ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/cboes-vpd-vpn-and-bxn-indices-rose-more-than-19-in-the-first-half-of-2023)). VolStrat's own test agrees. A 10% long-VIX-futures sleeve cut the S&P's Sep–Nov 2008 loss from −41.1% to −22.4%, but it cost 2.4 points of CAGR and left Sharpe unchanged at 0.54. On Growth, every volatility overlay lowered Sharpe, because Growth already de-risks in the same episodes (VolStrat backtests, data to 2026-11-03). Governance matters as much as the instrument. CalPERS ended its tail-hedge program in early 2020, citing "high cost", and missed more than $1 billion of gains that March ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1lft49hkwdjpv/CalPERS-CIO-Called-Out-By-Ex-Head-of-Tail-Risk-Program)). Spending and holding-period rules for any protection program have to be set before it starts.

Honest evaluation follows from these findings. The comparison benchmark must be the strategic portfolio *and* a static portfolio scaled to the overlay's average exposure, because otherwise lower average beta looks like skill ([Israelov](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/white-papers/pathetic-protection-the-elusive-benefits-of-protective-puts?aqrPDF=1); [Cederburg et al.](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)). Every variant tried counts toward a multiple-testing penalty. Options include a t-ratio hurdle above 3 ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592)), the Deflated Sharpe Ratio ([Bailey & López de Prado](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)) and the Probability of Backtest Overfitting ([Bailey et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)). Results should be shown as a distribution across rebalance offsets, not as a single path. No published standard exists for false-alarm cost or exit lag in regime signals; the checklist in the research notes is synthesized, not sourced. That is another gap VolStrat can fill.

## VolStrat's own record already reads as a drawdown dial

Every VolStrat result to date points the same way, and the pattern matches the literature closely enough that it should be treated as settled, not as a problem still to optimise away.

Two readings follow, and only the second should drive the product. The first is that VolStrat has not yet found the right design. The real-time studies above make that unlikely. My reading of them (an inference, not a published meta-estimate) is that a stocks-bonds-cash timer built on implied volatility should expect a Sharpe gain of no more than about 0.1 at equal risk in a good era, and close to nothing in a bad one. The second is that VolStrat measures risk accurately, and that accuracy is its value. Its regimes separate states whose forward volatility differs by a factor of three and whose 10%-loss odds differ by a factor of three. That is the input a PM needs for a risk budget, a stress test or a hedge decision. The "Growth" portfolio wraps this signal in two separate choices. The first is a leverage choice: 125% equity in Risk-On and 200% in Strong Risk-On. §15.4 showed that this "B" component raises return and drawdown together and leaves Sharpe unchanged. The second is a fixed defensive basket that failed in 2002 (utilities −29%) and in 2022 (bonds fell with stocks). Neither choice belongs to the signal, and a PM with their own strategic portfolio needs neither.

The era split is a finding to show, not a flaw to hide. Volatility signals detect shocks with V-shaped recoveries, and trend detects grinding bears. Goulding, Harvey and Mazzoleni show that the disagreement between fast and slow signals carries information in its own right ([JFE 2023](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:149:y:2023:i:3:p:378-406)). Baltussen et al. find that trend and defensive strategies protect at different stages of a drawdown ([FAJ 2026](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2026/best-defensive-strategies)). A PM is better served by two dials and a flag for when they disagree, such as "volatility calm, trend broken" in 2001–02 or "volatility in panic, fast trend turning up" in April 2020. A single blended score hides that state. v4 tried to merge the two and lost on both counts.

## A prioritised roadmap from allocator to risk-budget tool

### Stop claiming four things, keep claiming three

The first step costs nothing: change the claims. The table separates what the evidence supports from what the current presentation implies.

| Stop claiming (or implying) | Why | Replace with |
|---|---|---|
| "An allocation that beats benchmarks"; "Recommended allocation" on the Dashboard | Trails 60/40 in 1993–2006 (0.18 vs 0.51); v3 only ties in the European holdout (0.25 vs 0.25); beat the S&P's Sharpe in 57% of resamples | "Model risk budget" / "signal target", shown as a tilt from the user's strategic portfolio |
| Timing adds return | Same CAGR as the static mix at the same exposure; +0.4 pt/yr at equal risk; IC −0.06 | "Cuts drawdown at roughly equal return for the same average exposure" |
| High score means better returns ahead | Crisis days precede the highest returns; Sharpe is flat across regimes (§11); VRP literature ([BTZ 2009](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:22:y:2009:i:11:p:4463-4492)) | "High score means lower forward volatility and tail odds" |
| Leveraged Risk-On / Strong Risk-On as part of the edge | §15.4 "B" is a risk dial with unchanged Sharpe; levering in calm is where real-time volatility management fails ([Liu, Tang & Zhou](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/volatility-managed-portfolio-does-it-really-work/); [Bongaerts et al.](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2020/conditional-volatility-targeting)) | Leverage as an explicit, user-set band, off by default in overlay mode |

What to keep claiming, because the evidence supports it: **the score sorts risk** (3× forward volatility, 3× tail odds); **drawdown reduction is robust in shock-driven markets** (2007–2026 bootstrap), with the stated exception of grinding bears, where Growth's 1993–2006 drawdown (−50.1%) was deeper than the S&P's (−47.5%) and only adding trend fixed it (v4: −25.0%); and **the process is transparent and point-in-time**. Weights are published, data revisions are surfaced, tests are pre-registered and failures recorded, and the live records are append-only. That last point is the one most commercial tools cannot match.

Three design choices follow from this ordering. First, **overlay mode should start a new live record**, v3 or "overlay-v1", benchmarked against the strategic portfolio at equal average exposure, because §15.8 notes that the US history is now used up as test data. Any future claim of skill has to come from the forward record. Second, **default leverage in overlay mode should be near zero**, so the band is asymmetric. Liu, Tang and Zhou, and Bongaerts et al., both locate real-time failures in levering up in calm markets. VolStrat's own complacency-cap test touched only 97 Strong Risk-On days since 2007, too few to defend 2× on evidence. Third, **research on the signal itself should pause** until the forward record grows. The one remaining idea with a clear basis is a pre-registered "conditional, extremes-only" version (act only in the bottom and top score deciles), following Bongaerts et al. It would have to be judged on live data, not on 1993–2026 again.

With about ten major lows since 2007, statistical power on an information ratio will stay low for years. That is a statement about the evidence, not a reason to give up. It means VolStrat's credibility should rest on process (frozen rules, a full test log, surfaced revisions) and on risk diagnostics a PM can check against their own book, not on a Sharpe ratio waiting to become significant.

## Conclusion

The search for a VolStrat design that wins both eras is better treated as a measurement: the shape of the trade-off is the useful output. Volatility signals and trend signals fail in opposite regimes, and real-time studies show that merging them into one score averages away the information in their disagreement. A PM gains more from seeing "volatility calm, trend broken" than from a blended 55. A second finding follows from that. VolStrat's strongest asset is not its backtest but its honesty infrastructure: a frozen live record, a surfaced revision history, pre-registered pass/fail tests and published failures. The OFR and NFCI treat those features as the basis of trust, and the sell-side indicators cannot offer them.

Repositioned this way, VolStrat stops competing with 60/40, a contest it cannot reliably win, and becomes the risk-budget input that sits on top of 60/40 or any other strategic portfolio. Its success is then measured in units a PM already uses: drawdown avoided per basis point of return given up, information ratio within a tracking-error budget, false-alarm cost, and how quickly it re-risks after a low. These metrics also make its weaknesses visible instead of buried. On current evidence that is the most defensible way for a volatility-regime tool to earn a place in a PM's toolbox.
