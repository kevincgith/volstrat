# Implementing a Volatility/Risk-Regime Signal as a Portfolio Overlay: Mechanics, Hedging, Evaluation, Governance

Research date: 2026-11-04. Source-type labels: [Academic] peer-reviewed or working paper; [Practitioner] asset-manager or vendor research; [Vendor-index] index provider marketing or research; [Self-reported] manager's own performance claims; [Secondary] journalism, blogs, summaries; [Institutional] pension or government documents. Most papers were seen only as abstracts or summaries, not full text. Those items are flagged, and their numbers should be checked against the originals before publication.

## Overlay mechanics: tilts, drawdown/CPPI rules, vol targeting vs regime tilts, partial switching, tranching, turnover/cost budgets, tax drag

### Takeaway
The evidence favors partial, graduated, tranched exposure changes around a strategic benchmark over all-or-nothing switching. Volatility targeting improves risk-adjusted returns mainly for equity and credit and does little for bonds, currencies or commodities. Its in-sample alphas also shrink sharply in real-time implementations. Rebalance-timing luck alone can produce 100–400+ bps of annual dispersion, so staggered (tranched) execution is a cheap, well-supported design choice. In taxable accounts, turnover tax drag can wipe out typical TAA alpha.

### Cited Findings

**Volatility targeting (scaling exposure by inverse realized vol)**
- [Academic/Practitioner, JPM Fall 2018] Harvey, Hoyle, Korgaonkar, Rattray, Sargaison and Van Hemert (Man AHL/Duke), "The Impact of Volatility Targeting," study 60+ assets. Vol scaling improves Sharpe ratios for "risk assets" (equities, credit), which they attribute to the leverage effect. It has a negligible effect on Sharpe ratios for bonds, currencies and commodities. — [Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf); [Man Institute summary](https://www.man.com/maninstitute/the-impact-of-volatility-targeting)
- [Same paper] Across assets, vol targeting consistently lowers the probability of extreme returns and the volatility of volatility. It also cuts maximum drawdowns for balanced (60/40-style) and risk-parity portfolios. Because vol rises in sell-offs, vol targeting works partly like a momentum overlay. — [Man Institute](https://www.man.com/insights/the-impact-of-volatility-targeting)
- [Secondary summary of the same paper, by Larry Swedroe] For US equities, the Sharpe ratio rises from 0.40 unscaled to 0.48–0.51 vol-scaled. Vol of vol falls from 4.6% to 1.8%. *Not verified against the full paper.* — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/volatility-targeting-improves-risk-adjusted-returns/)
- [Academic, JFE 2020] Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang and Yan, "On the Performance of Volatility-Managed Portfolios," find that the in-sample spanning regressions behind vol-managed alphas cannot be implemented in real time. Reasonable out-of-sample versions "generally earn lower certainty equivalent returns and Sharpe ratios than do simple unscaled investments." Mean-variance implementations that add a vol-managed choice lower Sharpe ratios and CERs for 72 of 103 strategies. They attribute this to structural instability in the scaling parameters. — [Lehigh PDF](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:138:y:2020:i:1:p:95-117)
- [Academic, follow-up by Yan and coauthors] Managing on *downside* volatility gives positive alphas for 89 of 94 anomalies. So the real-time failure may be specific to the design of the total-vol scaling. — [Lehigh PDF "Downside"](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/Downside.pdf)

**Drawdown control / CPPI**
- [Academic] Drawdown constraints tend to produce a portfolio-insurance structure, which is effectively a put overlay on a constant-proportion strategy. — [Columbia drawdown-minimization paper](https://drupalgsb-test.paas.cc.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/6358/drawdownminimization.pdf)
- [Academic, EDHEC] Excess-drawdown control can be implemented through a hedging overlay that works around lock-ups, cash bans and liquidity limits. This is relevant when the underlying portfolio cannot be traded directly. — [EDHEC Risk](https://risk.edhec.edu/publications/dynamic-allocation-strategies-absolute-and)
- [Academic] Relative drawdown control targets underperformance versus the benchmark rather than absolute loss. This is a natural framing for an overlay on a strategic allocation. — [IOS Press, Algorithmic Finance](https://content.iospress.com/articles/algorithmic-finance/af040)
- [Practitioner, Lombard Odier 2019] Drawdown-control tools (vol targeting, CPPI) carry opportunity costs and should be handled with care. — [Lombard Odier](https://am.lombardodier.com/contents/news/white-papers/2019-1/july-1/drawdown-control-solutions-bewar.html)
- [Secondary/educational] CPPI sets risky exposure as a multiplier times the cushion above a floor. Frequent reallocation erodes the cushion. After a deep drawdown, CPPI can "cash-lock" and miss the recovery. — [MetricGate](https://metricgate.com/docs/constant-proportion-portfolio-insurance/); [brainbound.blog](https://www.brainbound.blog/constant-proportion-portfolio-insurance-cppi)

**Overlays with derivatives around a strategic allocation**
- [Institutional, Hampshire County Council pension fund paper] TAA is defined as varying asset-class weights above or below the fund's agreed strategic asset allocation. An "overlay" uses derivatives to adjust the asset allocation without trading the underlying managers. — [Hampshire decisions archive](https://www.hants.gov.uk/decisions/decisions-docs/070525-penfun-r0521144011)

**Rebalance timing luck and tranching (Newfound Research) [Practitioner]**
- "The Dumb (Timing) Luck of Smart Beta" (Nov 2019). For 100-stock, semi-annually rebalanced factor portfolios, timing luck is 100–400 bps a year, depending only on which rebalance dates are chosen. Estimates by factor: Momentum 4.4%, Enhanced Value 2.5%, Quality 2.0%, Low Vol 1.1%, and 0.5% for a 400-stock quarterly low-vol portfolio. 95% dispersion ranges run from ±1.4% to ±12.4%. One momentum variant was down 50.3% at a point when another was down only 13.8%. Higher turnover, less frequent rebalancing, more concentration and faster signals all increase timing luck. The remedy is overlapping or staggered portfolios (e.g., JAN-APR-JUL-OCT / FEB-MAY-AUG-NOV / MAR-JUN-SEP-DEC sleeves). — [Newfound, rebalance-timing-luck tag](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/tag/rebalance-timing-luck/)
- "Tranching, Trend, and Mean Reversion" (Apr 2020) gives a 2008–09 example of two 60/40 investors who rebalance annually, one in March and one in September, and end up with very different outcomes. Recommendation: rebalance "a little but frequently," e.g., correct 1/12 of the drift each month. Tranching can add a trend-like exposure at some horizons, and trading costs should be weighed against drift. — [Newfound](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/tag/rebalance-timing-luck/)
- "The Hidden Cost in Costless Put-Spread Collars: Rebalance Timing Luck" (Jan 2023). For a self-financing three-month put-spread collar, annualized tracking error between two versions that differ only in roll schedule exceeds 400 bps. — [Newfound](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/tag/rebalance-timing-luck/)
- [Practitioner, 2019] Shifting the rebalance day of a monthly multi-asset momentum model across 21 trading-day offsets gave annualized returns from 4.7% to 5.5%. — [Newfound, "Ensemble Multi-Asset Momentum"](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2019/07/ensemble-multi-asset-momentum/)
- [Secondary, 2025] Tranching a long/short strategy stabilizes results, at the cost of more frequent trading and order flow. — [Piinghel blog](https://piinghel.github.io/quants/2025/05/10/rebalancing-luck.html)

**Tax drag**
- [Practitioner, Research Affiliates "Tactical and Tax-Aware GTAA"] TAA alpha "can be easily exceeded by the increased taxes that result from profitable turnover." *Full text could not be retrieved: the RA URL now redirects after the restructuring into Syzygy Asset Management / VettaFi-RAFI.* — [RA PDF link](https://www.researchaffiliates.com/content/dam/ra/publications/pdf/713-tactical-and-tax-aware-gtaa.pdf)
- [Practitioner, Schwab] Modeled at a 40.8% short-term and 23.8% long-term rate: 5% turnover cuts returns by about 71 bps, and 100% turnover raises tax drag to about 194 bps a year. The model assumes turnover is random and spread evenly through the year. — [Schwab Center for Financial Research](https://www.schwab.com/resource/scfr-tax-drag)
- [Practitioner, GSAM 2025] For high-bracket investors in 60/40 portfolios with median turnover, tax drag could cut the desired allocation to tax-inefficient active vehicles by as much as 50%. — [Goldman Sachs Asset Management](https://am.gs.com/en-us/advisors/insights/article/2025/unlocking-tax-efficiency-tax-aware-approach-active-management)
- [Secondary, 2012, dated] Estimated tax drag of trend-based TAA was about 0.7–0.8% a year, described as "not terribly inefficient." — [MyPlanIQ](https://www.myplaniq.com/invest/september-24-2012-tax-efficient-investing/)

### Inferences
- A regime overlay should start as **graduated tilts** (e.g., steps of a few percentage points within a ±10–20% equity band) rather than binary risk-on/risk-off. Binary switching increases turnover, tax realization and timing luck (per Newfound's finding that faster, higher-turnover rules carry more timing luck), and it concentrates the outcome in a few decisions.
- Running the tilt through **futures or ETF overlays** on top of untouched strategic holdings separates the signal's P&L, limits realizations in the underlying managers, and lets the tilt be tranched (e.g., 1/4 of the target change per week). This is consistent with the Hampshire overlay definition and with Newfound's remedy.
- For multi-asset portfolios, the Man/Harvey results suggest scaling only the **equity/credit sleeve** on volatility, not bonds or commodities. Cederburg et al. warn that vol-targeting gains estimated in-sample may not survive real-time parameter estimation. Simple fixed rules (target vol, cap/floor on leverage) are more robust than optimized scaling coefficients.
- A turnover/cost budget can be set by reverse-engineering: if the expected gross IR × tracking error is X bps, transaction costs plus (for taxable investors) tax drag must stay well below X. Schwab's 71–194 bps tax-drag range is often larger than realistic TAA alpha (see the CEM evidence below).

- No primary source was found for typical institutional **tilt band widths** (e.g., ±5%, ±10%, ±20% equity). Policy-band conventions vary by Investment Policy Statement (IPS), and no survey was located.
- Section 1256 tax treatment of US index futures (blended 60% long-term / 40% short-term, marked to market) is relevant to futures overlays but was not verified with a primary IRS source this session.
- No quantitative **transaction-cost benchmarks** (bps per round trip for ES/MES futures, ETF spreads) were collected. They should be sourced separately (e.g., CME liquidity reports).
- The full text of RA's "Tactical and Tax-Aware GTAA" (authors, numbers) could not be retrieved.

## Tactical hedging: buying protection vs selling equity; cost of hedging programs; tail-hedge evidence; Cboe benchmarks

### Takeaway
The weight of independent and academic evidence says systematic, always-on put buying costs a lot over the long run and protects drawdowns worse than simply holding less equity, except in sudden crashes before expiry. Universa's strong claims are self-reported and rest on a hypothetical construction. Trend following and defensive factor/multi-asset strategies show the most robust protection per unit of cost in long-history studies. For a regime tool, de-risking (selling equity or futures) is the default action, and option protection is a crash-specific complement.

### Cited Findings
- [Academic/Practitioner, J. Alternative Investments 2019] Israelov (AQR), "Pathetic Protection: The Elusive Benefits of Protective Puts." Using the Cboe S&P 500 5% Put Protection Index (PPUT) and Monte Carlo simulations, he finds a "divested" portfolio (fixed lower equity weight plus cash) "nearly universally has better drawdown characteristics" than the put-protected portfolio. The volatility risk premium embedded in index options is the main cause. Puts win mainly when a very large crash occurs before expiry. — [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/white-papers/pathetic-protection-the-elusive-benefits-of-protective-puts?aqrPDF=1); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/pathetic-protection/); [CFA Digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2019/06/dig-v49-n6-2)
- [Academic/Practitioner, JPM 2019] Harvey, Hoyle, Rattray, Sargaison, Taylor and Van Hemert (Man), "The Best of Strategies for the Worst of Times," find that time-series trend strategies performed well across eight equity drawdowns and three recessions. Puts protected but at a large cost. A secondary commentary puts the put drag at about 8% a year. Short credit was cheaper than puts but still lost money over time. *The ~8% figure is unverified and comes from secondary commentary.* — [Scribd copy of JPM paper](https://www.scribd.com/document/491534322/jpm-2019-45-5-007); [the7circles commentary](https://the7circles.uk/?p=28982)
- [Academic, FAJ Vol 82 Issue 1, Jan 22, 2026] Baltussen, Martens and van der Linden, "The Best Defensive Strategies: Two Centuries of Evidence" (220+ years of data). Defensive equity factors (low-risk, quality, value) "consistently provide effective downside protection." Gold and put options "prove less drawdown or cost-effective." Trend following and a return-enhanced Defensive Absolute Return portfolio provide the most effective protection, and they are complementary across drawdown stages. *Abstract only; no figures retrieved.* — [CFA Institute RPC](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2026/best-defensive-strategies)
- [Self-reported, Universa via its ex-CalPERS program head Ron Lagnado, 2020] A hypothetical portfolio of a 3.33% Universa tail hedge plus 96.67% SPY beat the S&P 500's compound annual growth rate by 3.6 pts a year over about 12 years (about 47.9% more terminal wealth). Universa says a 60/40 proxy trailed the S&P by about 1 pt CAGR. Universa's fund reportedly returned 3,612% in March 2020. *Hypothetical, self-reported, and not independently audited.* — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1lft49hkwdjpv/CalPERS-CIO-Called-Out-By-Ex-Head-of-Tail-Risk-Program); [Advisor Perspectives headline](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2020/05/06/an-aqr-warning-and-a-3-612-return-fire-up-the-black-swan-debate)
- [Secondary, 2020] AQR's portfolio solutions group argued that headlines about March 2020 option gains skip the high long-run cost of these strategies. Universa responded that AQR analyzed generic OTM put baskets, not Universa's tail-hedged equity construction. — [Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2020/05/06/an-aqr-warning-and-a-3-612-return-fire-up-the-black-swan-debate); [Validea blog](https://blog.validea.com/?p=24021)
- [Institutional/Secondary] CalPERS ended its tail-risk hedging program (largest part run by Universa) in early 2020 and missed more than $1 billion in gains in March 2020. CIO Ben Meng cited "high cost, lack of scalability" and "better alternatives." Program size and cost were not disclosed in the article. — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1lft49hkwdjpv/CalPERS-CIO-Called-Out-By-Ex-Head-of-Tail-Risk-Program)
- [Secondary/independent] FactorResearch (Nicolas Rabener) compared tail-risk funds with cash-like assets over 2007–2020 and found similar risk-return ratios and drawdowns. — [Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2020/05/06/an-aqr-warning-and-a-3-612-return-fire-up-the-black-swan-debate)
- [Vendor-index, Cboe 2023] PPUT maximum drawdown over 37 years to June 2023 was 38.91%, versus 50.95% for the S&P 500. — [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/cboes-vpd-vpn-and-bxn-indices-rose-more-than-19-in-the-first-half-of-2023)
- [Vendor-index, Cboe Jul 2021] PPUT, CLL (95–110 collar) and CLLZ had betas below 0.75 over 35 years (Jun 1986–Jun 2021). The S&P 500 had 35 months with declines of 6% or more, versus 18 for PPUT. PPUT returned more than 10 pts above the comparison stock and commodity indices in 2008 and 2020. Annualized return and SD figures appear only in charts. — [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/benchmark-indices-series-hedging-downside-exposure-with-pput-cll-and-cllz-indices)
- [Vendor-index, Cboe 2012] Over about 25 years, CLL volatility was about 30% below the S&P 500's. A separate study put it at about 70% of S&P volatility over 26 years. — [Mondo Visione](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/new-study-compares-25-year-performance-of-options-strategy-benchmarks-to-traditi-2012214/); [Benzinga PR](https://www.benzinga.com/pressreleases/12/03/n2415443/new-study-finds-that-certain-options-and-futures-based-benchmark-indexe)
- [Vendor-index, Cboe Nov 2023] VXTH (buys 30-delta VIX calls on part of the portfolio) rose 54.9% in Q1 2020 while the S&P 500 TR fell 19.6%. In 2008, VXTH fell 19.3% versus about 37% for the S&P 500. — [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/cboe-vxth-index-had-5-year-gain-of-98-6-and-did-well-in-a-60-40-allocation); [Benzinga PR 2012](https://www.benzinga.com/pressreleases/12/03/n2415443/new-study-finds-that-certain-options-and-futures-based-benchmark-indexe)
- [Practitioner, Newfound 2023] Option-based hedges carry their own roll-timing luck: tracking error above 400 bps a year between collar variants that differ only in roll schedule. — [Newfound](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/tag/rebalance-timing-luck/)

### Inferences
- For a vol-regime signal, the economically cheapest response to an elevated-risk regime is usually to **reduce equity beta** (futures sale or ETF tilt), since Israelov shows de-risking dominates puts on drawdown per unit of cost. Options make sense when the regime signal specifically anticipates **gap/crash risk inside a short horizon**, or when the mandate forbids lowering exposure (e.g., benchmark-relative constraints).
- If options are used, buy them **conditionally** (only in flagged regimes, which also tend to be when implied vol is already high, so they cost more). Prefer spreads or collars to cut the premium, and stagger expiries to reduce the roll-timing luck Newfound documented.
- Cboe benchmark indices (PPUT, CLL, VXTH) are reasonable **cost-of-protection yardsticks**. A hedging program can be judged against "PPUT-equivalent" or "divested-equivalent" portfolios at equal average equity beta.
- The Universa/CalPERS episode shows a governance lesson: hedging programs are often cut after long bleed periods just before they pay off. The rules for hedging spend and holding period should be set in advance.

- No current (2025–2026) annualized return/volatility figures for PPUT, CLL or VXTH were found. Cboe index pages or DataShop are needed.
- No Research Affiliates paper on tail hedging was located in this session.
- Quantified cost of institutional hedging programs (bps of AUM per year) was not found in a primary source.

## Evaluation: how to judge a timing/regime tool honestly

### Takeaway
A regime tool should be judged against the strategic benchmark at equal average risk, with an information ratio and conditional performance by regime. Results should be adjusted for the number of variants tried, using a t ≥ 3 hurdle (Harvey-Liu-Zhu), the Deflated Sharpe Ratio, and the Probability of Backtest Overfitting. Implementation (timing luck, real-time parameter estimation) can be as large as the edge, and many in-sample timing gains vanish out of sample.

### Cited Findings
- [Academic, RFS 2016; NBER WP 20592] Harvey, Liu and Zhu, "…and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns." Given extensive data mining, a newly discovered factor should clear a t-ratio above 3.0, not 2.0. They conclude that most claimed research findings in financial economics are likely false. — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592); [RePEc RFS](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:29:y:2016:i:1:p:5-68.)
- [Academic, JPM 2014] Bailey and López de Prado, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio," correct a backtested Sharpe for selection bias under multiple testing and for non-normal returns. If only one trial was run, the DSR equals the Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio. — [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)
- [Academic, J. Computational Finance] Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu, "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting," estimate the probability that the in-sample-best configuration underperforms the median configuration out of sample, using combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV). — [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)
- [Academic, JFE 2020] Out-of-sample, real-time versions of volatility-managed strategies underperform unscaled ones for most of 103 strategies. This is a direct example of the in-sample-versus-real-time gap for vol-based timing. — [Lehigh PDF](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)
- [Practitioner, Newfound] Rebalance-date choice alone moves annual returns by 100–400 bps for many rule-based strategies, and by 4.7%–5.5% annualized across 21 offsets of a monthly momentum model. Any single backtest path is one draw from a timing-luck distribution. — [Newfound tag](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/tag/rebalance-timing-luck/); [Newfound 2019](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2019/07/ensemble-multi-asset-momentum/)
- [Academic, J. Alt. Investments 2019] Israelov's method compares protection to a "divested" portfolio at equivalent exposure, a model for **equal-risk comparisons**. Comparing a hedged or de-risked strategy to a fully invested benchmark without adjusting for risk is misleading. — [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/white-papers/pathetic-protection-the-elusive-benefits-of-protective-puts?aqrPDF=1)

### Inferences
- A suggested evaluation checklist built from the sources above:
  1. **Benchmark:** the strategic allocation, plus a static "equal-average-risk" version (strategic mix scaled to the overlay's average equity exposure), so timing skill is separated from a lower average beta.
  2. **Metrics:** information ratio vs. the strategic benchmark, tracking error, maximum relative drawdown, turnover and costs, and after-tax results where relevant.
  3. **Regime diagnostics:** hit rate of regime calls, mean forward returns and volatility conditional on each regime, false-positive cost (return forgone in "risk-off" periods that turned out benign), and lag at regime exits (the whipsaw cost of re-risking late).
  4. **Overfitting controls:** log every variant tried, and report the DSR and PBO across the family. Require t ≥ 3 for new signals, show the distribution over rebalance offsets (timing luck), and use walk-forward or real-time parameter estimation as Cederburg et al. require.
  5. **Pre-registration and live record:** freeze rules and parameters before an out-of-sample/live period, then judge the tool on that period alone. (The repo already contains docs/PREREG-v4.md, consistent with this practice.)
- A regime tool's live sample will be short and contain few regime transitions. Statistical power on the IR will be low for years, so governance must define in advance what evidence would lead to retirement.

### Gaps
- No primary source was retrieved on the **minimum backtest length** formula (Bailey and López de Prado) or on Harvey and Liu "Backtesting" haircut magnitudes (e.g., the share of Sharpe to haircut). Worth adding from the original papers.
- No published standard was found for "false-positive cost" metrics specific to regime signals. The checklist above is synthesized, not sourced.

## Governance and use in investment committees; how pension/OCIO TAA teams use risk regimes; published TAA performance evidence

### Takeaway
Large asset owners increasingly run a single reference portfolio plus an active-risk budget (the Total Portfolio Approach, used by CPP Investments, GIC, Australia's Future Fund, and CalPERS from 2026). A regime overlay fits that structure as one sized, budgeted source of active risk. Published evidence on TAA value added is thin and unflattering. Benchmarking data show total-fund active management adds only about 16 bps a year net, and TAA is a very small share of fund holdings.

### Cited Findings
- [Practitioner/Benchmarking, CEM 1992–2013, via IPE] Pension funds' total-fund gross value added averaged about 58 bps a year, falling to about 16 bps net of costs. Fees consumed nearly 75% of gross value added, and dispersion across funds was large. Value added came from TAA, manager selection and stock selection combined, not TAA alone. — [IPE](https://www.ipe.com/active-management-boosts-performance-by-16-basis-points-study-finds/10006836.article); [PlanSponsor](https://www.plansponsor.com/for-pensions-active-investing-pays-off/?layout=print)
- [Academic, Aglietta et al. 2012, using CEM data] TAA accounted for less than 1% of holdings, a very small part of pension portfolios. Policy allocation plays a minor role at the global level but a larger role within asset classes. — [Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/490513995/aglietta2012-pdf)
- [Secondary comment citing CEM/Beath 1998–2011 US DB study] The "TAA/hedge fund" asset class was reportedly the worst-performing asset class over the period, with net returns of 4.77% a year. *Secondhand, not verified in the original report.* — [CFA Institute comment](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/comment/reply/node/137301/field_comments/21961); [original NAREIT/CEM report](https://www.reit.com/sites/default/files/media/PDFs/AssetAllocationandFundPerformanceVersion2.pdf)
- [Secondary] CalPERS voted in November 2025 to adopt the Total Portfolio Approach, with implementation starting July 2026. It moves from 11 asset-class benchmarks to a single reference portfolio plus an active risk budget. — [iTiger news (aggregator)](https://www.itiger.com/news/1177183732)
- [Secondary] CPP Investments measures all value added against a simple reference portfolio of 85% global equities and 15% Canadian government bonds. GIC and CPP are early TPA adopters, and Australia's Future Fund has used TPA since 2006. Critics warn TPA can blur accountability for whether selection or allocation added value. — [Universal Asset Owners](https://www.universalassetowners.com/gic-vs-cpp/); [iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/1177183732)
- [Institutional, Hampshire CC] Local-authority pension committee papers describe TAA as deviations from the agreed strategic allocation, implemented directly or through a derivatives overlay. These deviations are subject to committee-approved ranges. — [Hampshire decisions](https://www.hants.gov.uk/decisions/decisions-docs/070525-penfun-r0521144011)
- [Institutional, CalPERS board agendas 2016] CalPERS investment committee materials on asset allocation and rebalancing policy exist as primary documents. *Fetched only as search results; contents not verified.* — [CalPERS item09a-04](https://www.calpers.ca.gov/docs/board-agendas/201602/invest/item09a-04.pdf); [CalPERS item08a-03](https://www.calpers.ca.gov/docs/board-agendas/201604/invest/item08a-03.pdf)
- [Institutional/Secondary] Case study of governance failure: CalPERS ended a tail-hedge program just before March 2020 and missed more than $1bn in gains. This led to public dispute between the CIO and the former program head. — [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1lft49hkwdjpv/CalPERS-CIO-Called-Out-By-Ex-Head-of-Tail-Risk-Program)

### Inferences
- In a TPA/reference-portfolio structure, a regime overlay should have: (a) a written **signal specification** (inputs, thresholds, rebalance schedule, tranche rules); (b) an explicit **tracking-error budget** and tilt band (e.g., the equity tilt capped at ±X% of the reference portfolio); (c) pre-defined **override rules**: who can override, documented rationale, and override P&L tracked separately from model P&L; (d) **escalation triggers**, e.g., relative drawdown beyond a threshold, signal outside its calibrated range, or data/feed failure; (e) a scheduled **review cadence** with pre-registered retirement criteria. Tracking override P&L separately addresses the accountability concern raised about TPA.
- Because the CEM evidence shows net value added from all active decisions is only about 16 bps a year, a regime overlay's expected net contribution should be framed modestly. The case for it is mainly **risk management** (drawdown and tail reduction at equal average risk), not alpha.
- The CalPERS episode argues for committing to a hedging or de-risking program for a minimum horizon and spend budget, with termination decisions tied to pre-agreed criteria rather than recent P&L.
